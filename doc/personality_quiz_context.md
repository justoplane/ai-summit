
# 16personalities.com Personality Types — Reference Guide

This document summarizes the framework used by 16personalities.com (the "NERIS" model) and describes each of the 16 personality types. It is written as background context for an LLM.

---

## 1. How the framework works

16personalities uses four-letter type codes borrowed from Myers-Briggs (MBTI) but does **not** use Jungian cognitive functions. Instead it measures five continuous trait scales ("aspects"). A person's result is a percentage on each scale; the letter shown is simply whichever side scored higher. The fifth scale (Identity) is unique to 16personalities and appended as a suffix (e.g., "INTJ-A", "ENFP-T").

### The five aspects

| Aspect             | Pole 1          | Pole 2          | What it measures                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | --------------- | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Mind**     | Introverted (I) | Extraverted (E) | How a person interacts with their environment and where they draw energy. Introverts prefer solitary or small-group activities, are drained by heavy social stimulation, and tend to be reserved. Extraverts seek social contact and external stimulation and are energized by it.                                                                                                                               |
| **Energy**   | Intuitive (N)   | Observant (S)   | How a person processes information. Intuitives are imaginative, curious about ideas and possibilities, and focused on patterns and the future. Observants are practical, grounded in concrete facts and present realities, and prefer proven methods.                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Nature**   | Thinking (T)    | Feeling (F)     | How a person makes decisions and handles emotions. Thinkers prioritize logic, objectivity, and effectiveness, and may seem detached. Feelers prioritize empathy, values, harmony, and the human impact of decisions.                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Tactics**  | Judging (J)     | Prospecting (P) | How a person approaches work, planning, and structure. Judgers like order, plans, closure, and clear rules. Prospectors prefer flexibility, spontaneity, keeping options open, and improvising.                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Identity** | Assertive (-A)  | Turbulent (-T)  | How confident a person is in their abilities and decisions, and how they handle stress. Assertives are self-assured, even-tempered, resistant to stress, and less likely to dwell on regrets or worry about others' opinions. Turbulents are self-conscious, sensitive to stress, perfectionistic, driven by a desire to improve, and prone to worry — but this often fuels motivation and attention to detail. |

Because these are spectrums, someone at 51% Introverted is very different from someone at 95% Introverted, even though both get an "I." The site emphasizes that the letters are a summary, not a box.

### The four Roles (groups of types)

Roles are defined by the Energy and Nature aspects (the middle two letters) and describe a person's general goals, interests, and preferred activities.

- **Analysts (NT)** — Intuitive + Thinking. Rational, strategic, independent, curious about systems and ideas, comfortable with debate, sometimes socially blunt. Types: INTJ, INTP, ENTJ, ENTP.
- **Diplomats (NF)** — Intuitive + Feeling. Empathetic, idealistic, drawn to meaning and human potential, diplomatic, cooperative, imaginative. Types: INFJ, INFP, ENFJ, ENFP.
- **Sentinels (SJ)** — Observant + Judging. Practical, dutiful, organized, stability-seeking, reliable, traditional, hardworking. Types: ISTJ, ISFJ, ESTJ, ESFJ.
- **Explorers (SP)** — Observant + Prospecting. Spontaneous, hands-on, adaptable, skilled at improvising and working with tools or people in the moment, thrill-seeking. Types: ISTP, ISFP, ESTP, ESFP.

### The four Strategies

Strategies combine the Mind aspect (I/E) with the Identity aspect (A/T) and describe how a person pursues goals and handles challenges.

- **Confident Individualism (I + A)** — Self-reliant, calm, prefers to do things alone, trusts own judgment, not driven by external validation. Risk: can be overly independent, ignore feedback, or under-engage socially.
- **People Mastery (E + A)** — Socially confident, comfortable in leadership, seeks connection, handles pressure well. Risk: can overlook problems, be overconfident, or dominate.
- **Constant Improvement (I + T)** — Quiet, introspective, perfectionistic, driven by a wish to do better, sensitive to criticism, prone to self-doubt and stress. Risk: anxiety, overthinking, avoidance.
- **Social Engagement (E + T)** — Outgoing but sensitive to others' opinions, energetic, ambitious, emotionally expressive, motivated by fear of failure and desire for approval. Risk: impulsiveness, emotional volatility, over-reliance on validation.

### Naming convention

Each type has a nickname (e.g., "The Architect"). The full result looks like: **Type Name (CODE-Identity)**, e.g., *Advocate (INFJ-T)*.

## 2. The 16 Types

For each type: core description, strengths, weaknesses, and notes on relationships, work, and how the A/T suffix tends to show up.

---

### ANALYSTS (NT)

---

#### INTJ — "The Architect"

**Overview:** Strategic, independent, and intensely logical thinkers who combine imagination with decisiveness. INTJs are long-range planners who enjoy understanding how complex systems work and improving them. They are rare, private, and often skeptical of convention, authority, and emotional reasoning. They value competence above nearly everything and hold themselves and others to high standards. They tend to be confident in their intellect but can appear aloof, arrogant, or coldly critical.

**Strengths:** Rational and quick to grasp complex ideas; informed and well-read; independent and self-directed; determined and hardworking toward chosen goals; curious and open to new ideas if logically sound; original thinkers who see novel solutions.

**Weaknesses:** Arrogant or dismissive of those they see as less capable; overly analytical and insensitive to emotional context; combative and prone to treating disagreements as debates to win; socially clueless or impatient with small talk; can be overly perfectionistic and slow to trust; may dismiss valuable input from "non-logical" sources.

**Relationships:** Seek a partner who is an intellectual equal; loyal but not overtly romantic; may struggle with emotional expression and need to work at empathy.

**Work:** Thrive in roles requiring strategy, analysis, and autonomy (engineering, science, law, strategy, architecture, software). Dislike micromanagement, inefficiency, and office politics. As leaders they set clear visions but can be demanding and impersonal.

**A vs T:** INTJ-A is confident, unbothered by criticism, decisive. INTJ-T is more self-critical, second-guesses plans, works harder to prove themselves, more sensitive to others' opinions than they admit.

---

#### INTP — "The Logician"

**Overview:** Curious, inventive, and analytical, INTPs are drawn to theories, patterns, and abstract problems. They enjoy exploring ideas for their own sake and are often described as absent-minded intellectuals. They question everything, are skeptical of accepted wisdom, and prize intellectual honesty. They tend to be relaxed and unconventional, but can withdraw into their own thoughts and neglect practical matters, deadlines, and social obligations.

**Strengths:** Analytical and logical; original thinkers who generate unusual ideas; open-minded and willing to change their views given evidence; curious across many domains; objective and fair; honest and direct.

**Weaknesses:** Disconnected or distant, may seem uninterested in others; insensitive and prone to unintentionally hurtful bluntness; dissatisfied, always finding flaws; impatient with routine and details; perfectionistic to the point of never finishing; prone to procrastination and second-guessing; may struggle with commitment and follow-through.

**Relationships:** Value honesty and intellectual connection; may be hesitant to initiate; can neglect emotional maintenance and need reminders to express affection.

**Work:** Best suited to research, science, programming, mathematics, philosophy, and roles allowing independent problem-solving. Dislike rigid structure, repetitive tasks, and heavy social demands. Not usually drawn to management.

**A vs T:** INTP-A is more self-assured and less bothered by uncertainty. INTP-T is more anxious about being wrong, more perfectionistic, more likely to spiral in analysis-paralysis.

---

#### ENTJ — "The Commander"

**Overview:** Natural leaders who are bold, strategic, and strong-willed. ENTJs combine charisma and confidence with a drive to achieve ambitious goals. They think in terms of long-term plans and efficiency, and they enjoy organizing people and resources to accomplish objectives. They are decisive, energetic, and enjoy challenges, but can be domineering, impatient, and dismissive of emotions — their own and others'.

**Strengths:** Efficient and results-oriented; energetic and driven; self-confident; strong-willed and persistent; strategic thinkers who see the big picture; charismatic and inspiring; decisive.

**Weaknesses:** Stubborn and dominant, may steamroll others; intolerant of perceived incompetence or emotional arguments; impatient; arrogant; poor at handling emotions and may seem cold or ruthless; can prioritize goals over people.

**Relationships:** Expect growth and effort from partners; direct communicators; loyal and take responsibility, but need to develop patience and emotional attentiveness.

**Work:** Excel as executives, entrepreneurs, project leads, lawyers, consultants, and in any role rewarding leadership and strategy. Frustrated by bureaucracy, inefficiency, and lack of authority.

**A vs T:** ENTJ-A is unflappable and confident to the point of overconfidence. ENTJ-T is more driven by fear of failure, more self-critical, and can be more reactive under stress.

---

#### ENTP — "The Debater"

**Overview:** Quick-witted, clever, and argumentative in the best sense, ENTPs love intellectual sparring and challenging ideas. They are idea generators who enjoy exploring possibilities and poking holes in assumptions. Charismatic and energetic, they can also be contrarian, insensitive, and easily bored — starting many projects but finishing few. They thrive on novelty and mental stimulation.

**Strengths:** Knowledgeable and quick learners; quick thinkers with rapid mental connections; original and creative; excellent brainstormers; charismatic and engaging; energetic; comfortable with debate and dissent.

**Weaknesses:** Very argumentative, may argue for sport in ways that alienate people; insensitive to feelings; intolerant of those who won't engage in debate; difficulty focusing and following through; dislike practical details and routine; may seem unreliable.

**Relationships:** Fun, engaging, and growth-oriented partners; can be inconsistent or neglect emotional needs; need to learn when not to debate.

**Work:** Suited to entrepreneurship, consulting, law, marketing, engineering, journalism — roles rewarding creativity and adaptability. Dislike rigid hierarchies and repetitive tasks.

**A vs T:** ENTP-A is confident and carefree. ENTP-T is more self-doubting, more affected by criticism, and may seek validation despite an assertive front.

---

### DIPLOMATS (NF)

---

#### INFJ — "The Advocate"

**Overview:** Idealistic, principled, and deeply empathetic, INFJs are driven by a sense of purpose and a desire to help others and improve the world. They are often described as the rarest type. They are insightful about people, private, and quietly determined. They combine a rich inner life and strong convictions with a warm, gentle exterior. They can be perfectionistic, sensitive to criticism, prone to burnout from overextending themselves, and may withdraw abruptly ("door-slamming") when hurt.

**Strengths:** Creative and imaginative; insightful about motivations and hidden dynamics; principled and driven by values; passionate about causes; altruistic and generous; determined once committed.

**Weaknesses:** Sensitive to criticism and conflict; extremely private, hard to get to know; perfectionistic about themselves and their causes; may avoid the ordinary or practical; prone to burnout; can be stubborn about ideals.

**Relationships:** Seek deep, authentic connections; loyal and devoted; may idealize partners and be hurt when reality falls short; need partners who respect their need for alone time.

**Work:** Drawn to counseling, psychology, writing, teaching, healthcare, nonprofit work, and any career aligned with their values. Dislike high-conflict or purely profit-driven environments.

**A vs T:** INFJ-A is more at peace with imperfection and less reactive to criticism. INFJ-T is more self-critical, more anxious about others' perceptions, more prone to emotional exhaustion.

---

#### INFP — "The Mediator"

**Overview:** Gentle, imaginative, and idealistic, INFPs are guided by strong inner values and a desire for authenticity. They are creative, often drawn to writing and the arts, and deeply empathetic — they see the good in people and seek harmony. They can appear shy or reserved but are passionate underneath. They struggle with practical matters, self-criticism, and can feel misunderstood or overwhelmed by the gap between their ideals and reality.

**Strengths:** Empathetic and compassionate; generous; open-minded and tolerant; creative and imaginative; passionate about values and causes; idealistic in ways that inspire others; dedicated once committed.

**Weaknesses:** Unrealistic or overly idealistic; self-isolating; unfocused and easily distracted; emotionally vulnerable and prone to taking things personally; too self-critical; may avoid conflict at their own expense; can struggle with practical tasks and data.

**Relationships:** Deeply romantic, seek soulmate-level connection; may idealize partners; need emotional honesty and time alone; conflict-averse.

**Work:** Suited to writing, art, counseling, social work, education, psychology, and mission-driven work. Dislike competitive, cutthroat, or highly structured corporate environments.

**A vs T:** INFP-A is calmer, more self-accepting. INFP-T is more emotionally intense, self-doubting, and sensitive to criticism, often driven by a desire to become "better."

---

#### ENFJ — "The Protagonist"

**Overview:** Warm, charismatic, and inspiring, ENFJs are natural leaders who are driven to help others grow. They are highly attuned to people's emotions and skilled at bringing groups together. They are idealistic, reliable, and often take on the role of mentor or organizer. They can be overly idealistic, too eager to please, and may struggle to make decisions that disappoint others or to set boundaries. They may also become intense or preachy about their values.

**Strengths:** Receptive and open to others' views; reliable; passionate; altruistic; charismatic and persuasive; natural leaders; tolerant and inclusive.

**Weaknesses:** Unrealistic idealism; overly sensitive to criticism; condescending when they assume they know best; intense; may take on others' problems excessively; struggle with self-care and boundaries; fluctuating self-esteem based on others' approval.

**Relationships:** Devoted, communicative, growth-oriented partners; may over-invest and neglect their own needs; sensitive to conflict.

**Work:** Excel in teaching, coaching, HR, counseling, politics, management, public relations — roles involving people development and communication. Struggle in impersonal or highly competitive settings.

**A vs T:** ENFJ-A is confident and steady in leadership. ENFJ-T is more concerned with approval, more likely to take criticism to heart, more anxious about letting people down.

---

#### ENFP — "The Campaigner"

**Overview:** Enthusiastic, creative, sociable free spirits who see life as full of possibilities. ENFPs are warm, expressive, curious about people and ideas, and skilled at making connections between them. They are optimistic and energetic and dislike routine. They can struggle with follow-through, overthinking, emotional intensity, and stress when they feel constrained or unappreciated.

**Strengths:** Curious and observant; energetic and enthusiastic; excellent communicators; know how to relax and have fun; very popular and friendly; imaginative; good at reading people.

**Weaknesses:** Poor practical skills and follow-through; difficulty focusing; overthinking and reading too much into things; easily stressed and emotionally overwhelmed; highly emotional; independent to a fault; may seek approval too much.

**Relationships:** Warm, spontaneous, affectionate; want deep connection and growth; may struggle with routine or long-term consistency.

**Work:** Suited to creative fields, entrepreneurship, counseling, teaching, journalism, marketing, and people-oriented roles. Dislike rigid structure, repetition, and micromanagement.

**A vs T:** ENFP-A is confident and buoyant. ENFP-T is more emotionally volatile, more sensitive to disapproval, more prone to stress and self-doubt.

---

### SENTINELS (SJ)

---

#### ISTJ — "The Logistician"

**Overview:** Practical, fact-minded, and dependable, ISTJs value integrity, duty, and order. They are thorough, methodical, and take responsibilities seriously. They rely on proven methods and established rules, and they expect others to do the same. They are reserved and prefer to work quietly. They can be rigid, judgmental, resistant to change, and uncomfortable with emotions.

**Strengths:** Honest and direct; strong-willed and dutiful; very responsible; calm and practical; create and enforce order; excellent with facts and details; reliable.

**Weaknesses:** Stubborn; insensitive, may hurt feelings with blunt facts; always by the book, uncomfortable with unorthodox approaches; judgmental; may blame themselves unreasonably; resistant to change and new ideas.

**Relationships:** Loyal and committed; express love through actions rather than words; may struggle with emotional expression and spontaneity.

**Work:** Excel in accounting, administration, law enforcement, military, logistics, auditing, data analysis, and any role rewarding accuracy and reliability. Dislike ambiguity and constant change.

**A vs T:** ISTJ-A is composed and confident in their methods. ISTJ-T is more self-critical, more anxious about mistakes, and more affected by others' opinions.

---

#### ISFJ — "The Defender"

**Overview:** Warm, dedicated, and protective, ISFJs are quietly devoted to the people and institutions they care about. They are practical, observant, meticulous, and supportive, often working behind the scenes. They combine strong caretaking instincts with attention to detail and a respect for tradition. They can be overly modest, take on too much, repress their own feelings, and resist change.

**Strengths:** Supportive; reliable and patient; imaginative and observant in practical ways; enthusiastic about helping; loyal and hardworking; good practical skills.

**Weaknesses:** Humble and shy, undervalue their contributions; take things personally; repress feelings; overload themselves; reluctant to change; too altruistic, may be taken advantage of.

**Relationships:** Deeply committed and nurturing; may struggle to voice their own needs; sensitive to conflict and criticism.

**Work:** Well suited to nursing, healthcare, teaching, social work, administration, customer service, and roles with clear structure and a helping element. Dislike conflict-heavy or chaotic environments.

**A vs T:** ISFJ-A is steadier and more self-assured. ISFJ-T is more anxious about pleasing others, more prone to worry and self-criticism.

---

#### ESTJ — "The Executive"

**Overview:** Organized, traditional, and decisive, ESTJs are natural administrators who value order, structure, and clear rules. They take charge, set expectations, and hold people accountable. They are practical, hardworking, and loyal to institutions and community. They can be inflexible, judgmental, uncomfortable with unconventional ideas, and dismissive of emotions.

**Strengths:** Dedicated; strong-willed; direct and honest; loyal, patient, and reliable; enjoy creating order; excellent organizers and managers.

**Weaknesses:** Inflexible and stubborn; uncomfortable with unconventional situations; judgmental; too focused on social status or appearances; difficulty relaxing; difficulty expressing emotion; may be harsh critics.

**Relationships:** Committed and traditional; expect responsibility and follow-through; may need to soften bluntness and attend to emotional needs.

**Work:** Thrive as managers, administrators, project managers, military officers, financial officers, and in law, business, and government. Dislike disorder and inefficiency.

**A vs T:** ESTJ-A is confident and unbothered by pushback. ESTJ-T is more sensitive to criticism, more stressed by failures, and may be harsher on themselves and others under pressure.

---

#### ESFJ — "The Consul"

**Overview:** Caring, sociable, and community-minded, ESFJs are attentive to others' needs and eager to help. They value harmony, tradition, and social connection, and they often act as hosts and organizers. They are practical, loyal, and sensitive to social dynamics. They can be overly concerned with status and approval, inflexible, vulnerable to criticism, and needy for validation.

**Strengths:** Strong practical skills; strong sense of duty; very loyal; sensitive and warm; good at connecting with others; organized.

**Weaknesses:** Worried about social status; inflexible; reluctant to innovate or improvise; vulnerable to criticism; too needy or dependent on validation; too selfless, may neglect themselves.

**Relationships:** Warm, attentive, devoted partners; value tradition and commitment; sensitive to conflict and rejection.

**Work:** Suited to healthcare, teaching, social work, HR, event planning, customer relations, and community roles. Dislike impersonal, conflict-driven, or highly abstract work.

**A vs T:** ESFJ-A is more self-assured and less dependent on approval. ESFJ-T is more anxious about relationships and reputation, more reactive to criticism.

---

### EXPLORERS (SP)

---

#### ISTP — "The Virtuoso"

**Overview:** Practical, hands-on experimenters who love understanding how things work by taking them apart. ISTPs are calm, rational, adaptable, and independent. They enjoy tools, machines, and physical skills, and they thrive on tangible problem-solving. They are reserved and value personal freedom. They can be stubborn, insensitive, easily bored, risk-prone, and commitment-averse.

**Strengths:** Optimistic and energetic; creative and practical; spontaneous and rational; know how to prioritize; great in a crisis; relaxed.

**Weaknesses:** Stubborn; insensitive; private and reserved; easily bored; dislike commitment; risky behavior; may seem detached or unemotional.

**Relationships:** Loyal but need space; not naturally emotionally expressive; prefer shared activities over deep talks.

**Work:** Suited to engineering, mechanics, trades, forensics, piloting, emergency services, IT, athletics, and roles combining action with technical skill. Dislike rigid rules and abstract theorizing.

**A vs T:** ISTP-A is cool under pressure and highly independent. ISTP-T is more restless, more self-critical, and more affected by others' judgments.

---

#### ISFP — "The Adventurer"

**Overview:** Gentle, artistic, and spontaneous, ISFPs live in the moment and express themselves through action and aesthetics. They are open-minded, sensitive, and value personal freedom and authenticity. They are often skilled in art, design, music, or craft. They tend to avoid conflict and long-term planning and can be unpredictable, easily stressed, overly competitive, and prone to low self-esteem.

**Strengths:** Charming; sensitive to others; imaginative and artistic; passionate; curious; adaptable.

**Weaknesses:** Fiercely independent; unpredictable; easily stressed; overly competitive; fluctuating self-esteem; dislike planning and abstract thinking; may avoid conflict rather than resolve it.

**Relationships:** Warm, affectionate, and supportive; need space and freedom; sensitive to criticism; may not express needs directly.

**Work:** Suited to art, design, music, photography, culinary work, healthcare, veterinary work, and hands-on caregiving. Dislike rigid schedules and high-pressure corporate environments.

**A vs T:** ISFP-A is more relaxed and self-accepting. ISFP-T is more self-conscious, more emotionally volatile, and more affected by stress.

---

#### ESTP — "The Entrepreneur"

**Overview:** Bold, energetic, perceptive, and action-oriented, ESTPs love living on the edge and being the center of attention. They are quick thinkers who excel in dynamic environments and enjoy risk, competition, and immediate results. They are practical, direct, and socially confident. They can be impatient, insensitive, unstructured, risk-prone, and inclined to ignore rules or long-term consequences.

**Strengths:** Bold; rational and practical; original; perceptive of others' motives and changes; direct; sociable.

**Weaknesses:** Insensitive; impatient; risk-prone; unstructured, dislike rules; may miss the bigger picture; defiant toward authority.

**Relationships:** Fun, spontaneous, and affectionate; may struggle with long-term commitment or emotional depth.

**Work:** Suited to sales, entrepreneurship, emergency services, sports, marketing, law enforcement, and roles rewarding quick action and persuasion. Dislike bureaucracy and routine.

**A vs T:** ESTP-A is confident and unfazed. ESTP-T is more reactive, more sensitive to setbacks, and may be more impulsive under stress.

---

#### ESFP — "The Entertainer"

**Overview:** Spontaneous, outgoing, and fun-loving, ESFPs love excitement, people, and aesthetic experiences. They are warm, generous, and observant, and they thrive when performing, hosting, or helping others enjoy themselves. They are practical and grounded in the present. They can be easily bored, conflict-avoidant, poor at long-term planning, sensitive to criticism, and prone to distraction.

**Strengths:** Bold; original; aesthetically minded; practical; observant; excellent people skills.

**Weaknesses:** Sensitive to criticism; conflict-averse; easily bored; poor long-term planners; unfocused; may overindulge or avoid responsibility.

**Relationships:** Affectionate, fun, and attentive; may avoid serious conversations or long-term planning.

**Work:** Suited to entertainment, hospitality, sales, event planning, teaching young children, fashion, and social roles. Dislike solitary, repetitive, or heavily analytical work.

**A vs T:** ESFP-A is upbeat and confident. ESFP-T is more emotionally sensitive, more concerned with approval, and more prone to stress.

## 4. Notes for interpreting results

- **The suffix matters.** "-A" and "-T" versions of the same type can behave quite differently under stress and in how they seek validation. Turbulent types are generally more anxious and perfectionistic; Assertive types are calmer and more self-assured (sometimes to the point of complacency).
- **Percentages matter more than letters.** A result near 50% on any scale means that trait is not strongly expressed and the person may show behaviors from both sides.
- **Not clinical or scientifically validated.** 16personalities is a popular self-report tool, not a validated psychological instrument. Types describe tendencies and preferences, not fixed traits or abilities, and results can shift over time or with mood.
- **No type is better than another.** Each type has characteristic strengths and blind spots. When using a type to tailor communication, it is most useful for adjusting tone, level of detail, structure, and emotional framing rather than making assumptions about what a person can or cannot do.
- **Practical use for an LLM:** If given a user's type, adjust accordingly — e.g., give NT types logic, options, and efficiency; give NF types meaning, empathy, and values framing; give SJ types structure, clear steps, and reliability; give SP types concise, practical, actionable content. Introverts may prefer less chit-chat; extraverts may enjoy a more conversational style. Turbulent types may benefit from reassurance; Assertive types from directness.
